Propelon inbound engineA GTM engineering case study
Executive summary

Every person who writes to Propelon gets a fast, careful reply.

Propelon is an early-stage company that sells the management system for turning AI investment into enterprise value. I built the engine behind its website forms: when someone writes in, it finds their company, decides who should answer, and prepares a reply for a person to review within one business day. This page tells the story in order: the problem, what I built, how it works, and what I learned.

The project at a glance

One enquiry, start to finishA person sends. The engine never does.
01 · The company

Propelon helps large enterprises turn AI spending into results they can prove.

Propelon is an early-stage company. Its management system puts every AI initiative through gates that can say no, and ends each one in a figure the finance function will sign. Customers arrive through an executive briefing, a demo, a workshop and a proposal.

02 · The problem

Enquiries are rare, each is a first impression, and what may be said is tightly limited.

Replies must never state a price, claim a certification the company does not hold, or cite an unsourced figure. Doing this by hand risks slow, inconsistent or non-compliant replies, and quiet interest from target accounts goes unseen.

03 · What I built

A rules-driven engine that prepares every response and stops short of sending it.

It identifies the organization behind each enquiry, routes it by written rules, prepares a research note and a draft, screens every statement against the claims policy, and hands the result to a person. It also scores organization-level interest and gives the team one daily view.

04 · Why it matters

Faster, more consistent replies, with nothing the company cannot stand behind.

Every decision is explainable and versioned. The rules live in plain files that the team can own after the engagement ends. The system is built and tested end to end; going live with real enquiries is the next step.

1 · Why it was needed

Four questions decide whether a first reply helps or hurts.

Who is this organization? Who owns the reply? What may we say? When is it due? Left to chance, each enquiry has to answer all four from scratch. Watch one enquiry travel through both worlds.

Without the engineEach enquiry is a fresh puzzle

It lands in a shared inbox. Someone reads it when they can, guesses whether it is a buyer, and writes a reply from scratch with no deadline and no record of why.

With the engineThe four questions are already answered

The organization is matched, the route is set by written rules, the draft is built from approved facts and screened, the clock is running, and a person reviews and sends.

The point of the timing: the best moment to build the process is before the first real enquiry arrives, not after a slow or careless reply has already been sent.
